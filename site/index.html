Tale of the tape

What the bots are built from. The biases are the ones meteorologists actually complain about.

Big GusGlobal Forecast System · NOAA/NCEP, College Park, MD
Grid
~13 km, global, FV3 core
Reach
384 hours (16 days), four runs a day
Entourage
31-member GEFS ensemble
  • Shifts winter storm tracks too far northwest late in the run
  • Convective spin-up: too much precipitation in the first hours, which can pollute totals downstream
  • Flip-flops run to run; a solution held for four runs feels like a vow
  • GEFS is underdispersive: the members hug the control, so the spread understates real uncertainty
  • Tends to be stingy on heavy-rain totals, except when the convection scheme runs wild
Quirk: the weathervane on his head never stops turning. In the Blizzard of 2026 his 12z Tuesday run put the bomb 85 km from where it verified, six days out. Then he lost it for four runs.
Eccy the EuroECMWF IFS · Reading, UK (compute in Bologna)
Grid
~9 km, global
Reach
240 h full runs at 00 and 12 UTC, 15-day ensemble
Entourage
51-member EPS, plus the AIFS little brother
  • Too dry in heavy-precipitation events, including East Coast bombs
  • Too wet in light-precipitation setups: paints drizzle where there is none
  • Summer surface warm bias, roughly 3–5 °F over the continents
  • Tropical stratosphere runs cold; summer soils run cold
  • Best average verification scores of the three, which is exactly why a miss stings
Quirk: insists on hectopascals, charges for the full data feed, and only truly shows up twice a day. His AI sibling out-forecast him for the first five days of this storm, and he will not discuss it.
Little NammyNorth American Mesoscale · NOAA/NCEP
Grid
12 km parent with a 3 km CONUS nest
Reach
84 hours (the nest stops at 60), four runs a day
Entourage
None. Borrows his starting conditions from the GFS analysis
  • Over-forecasts precipitation in cold air, so snow totals balloon
  • Daytime surface temperatures run too cool
  • Holds surface moisture instead of mixing it out, inflating dew points and CAPE
  • Code frozen since 2017; slated for retirement when the RRFS takes over
  • The 3 km nest is often good at where the bands set up, even when the totals are high
Quirk: can't see past 84 hours, so he misses the first three rounds of every long-range argument and then arrives shouting.
Storm bouts are built from WPC's probabilistic winter precipitation forecasts, GFS and NAM runs from NOAA Open Data on AWS, ECMWF IFS and AIFS open data, Stage IV precipitation and NWS climate reports via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet, and WPC's archived discussions. Exhibition bouts use illustrative numbers. The bots are original characters; their opinions are scripted. Voices use your browser's speech engine.
